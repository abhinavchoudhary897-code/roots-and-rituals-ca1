Explore official TRIFED resources

TRIFED supports procurement and marketing of tribal products through retail and exhibitions. This website does not sell products or certify fair trade.

Know the maker

Ask who made the artwork, where it originated and how its provenance is documented. Do not confuse a craft-level registration or an artist award with an individual product certificate.

Value skilled labour

Consider the time, materials, training and craftsmanship involved rather than judging handmade work only by its price. Seek a transparent explanation of how payment reaches the artisan.

Support responsible markets

Explore recognised artisan cooperatives, exhibitions and official tribal marketing channels. Respect cultural restrictions and avoid copying ceremonial designs without permission.

TRIFED craft categories

Dhurrie weaving

Dhurries are flat-woven floor coverings made with interlaced warp and weft threads, often using cotton or wool. Regional weaving traditions vary in their materials, designs and tools. The panja, a comb-like tool, is used to pack the weft in some styles.

From yarn to textile

Preparing the yarn, arranging the warp, weaving the pattern and finishing the edges require time and skill. Supporting traditional textile markets can help preserve this knowledge. Dhurrie weaving is included as additional craft context, not as a sixth tribal category or a practice exclusive to tribal communities.
